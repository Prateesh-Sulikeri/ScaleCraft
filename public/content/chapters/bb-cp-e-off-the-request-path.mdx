Group E's three exercises each moved one kind of work off the request path, on
a board where the previous chapter's fix was already in place. This
checkpoint starts from a system where none of it has been done. Everything
happens while a diner waits for a booking to confirm, and the booking takes
3.4 seconds because of it.

The skill being tested is sorting, before drawing anything. A brief like this
one mixes four kinds of work in one list: a job that follows a request, news
that several teams need, work nobody asked for, and a burst that arrives at the
front door. Each has a different shape in Group E. Getting the shapes right is
most of the design; drawing them is the easy part.

## Your turn

**The product.** A restaurant-reservations platform. Diners search restaurants
and book tables; restaurants manage their tables from a till system and a
tablet. Everything on the canvas answers a diner while they wait, and right now
everything else is crammed into that same path.

What it has to do:

- Booking a table answers in under 300 ms. The confirmation text goes through
  an SMS provider that takes 2 to 5 seconds and sometimes fails for an hour.
  It must not slow the booking down, and no confirmation may be silently lost.
- Every confirmed booking matters to three teams: the restaurant's tablet shows
  it, the loyalty programme awards points for it, and search's "tables free
  tonight" count has to drop by one. Each needs every booking. A fourth team,
  analytics, starts next quarter, and the booking code must not change when it
  does.
- At 03:00 every night, no-shows are counted and restaurants are billed for
  them. It runs exactly once a night, however many application instances are
  running.
- When dinner service opens, restaurants' till systems push table-status
  updates in a burst: 40% of a day's writes in fifteen minutes. That burst must
  not decide how big the diner-facing pool is, and handling it should cost
  nothing the rest of the day.

**What you are not told**: how many new components there are, which kind of
hand-off each piece of work needs, or where each new path starts. More than one
component can answer some of these lines; Submit accepts any taught shape that
meets the requirement. Watch the line styles as you draw: the canvas only lets
you use the dashed kind where a hand-off is genuinely one-way.
